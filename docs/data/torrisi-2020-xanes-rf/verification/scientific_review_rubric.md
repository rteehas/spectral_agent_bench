# Scientific adjudication for the open-investigation questions (version 3)

The numerical verifier is an integrity gate. Its successful result is `scientific_review_required`, with `scientific_pass: null`. It checks released-row identities, target labels, declared training/test partitions and test-prediction coverage, and calculates prediction metrics. Shared material IDs and missing IDs are reported for review. Optional explicit claims about material separation or paired comparisons are checked when supplied. Experiment manifests and metric files are not required. This result is never a scientific pass.

A final evaluator must review and execute the submitted source, independently validate the supporting evidence, and assess the question-specific criteria below. The archived forests and published tables supply scientific context and examples of results; they are not acceptance thresholds. Alternative filtering, splitting, estimators, tuning, representations, repetitions, and uncertainty procedures are allowed when they support the research claim. A sound negative or mixed result can pass. A submission need not reproduce a particular score, class-balancing method, feature ranking, seed, forest, or conclusion.

## Required independent review

Record an evidence path or source location and a reason for each criterion. Use `satisfied`, `unsupported`, `incorrect`, or `not_applicable` for individual criteria. Record a final `pass`, `fail`, or `incomplete`. A numerical failure blocks scientific acceptance; successful arithmetic leaves the review incomplete until the applicable substantive criteria have been assessed. A final pass requires all applicable criteria to be satisfied. `not_applicable` cannot waive a required scientific comparison.

1. **The source generates the claimed artifacts.** Execute the submitted reproduction command in a clean, controlled environment containing the supplied inputs and declared dependencies. Regenerate the partitions, predictions, reported quantitative evidence and figures. Compare them with the submission. For deterministic experiments require numerical agreement with a justified floating-point tolerance. If a method is intentionally stochastic, evaluate the declared reproducibility procedure and show that the regenerated results support the claimed uncertainty and conclusions. Do not execute submission code inside the numerical verifier itself. Merely including a script or command does not satisfy this criterion.
2. **The model learns from permissible evidence.** Inspect feature generation, fitting, tuning, selection, and every data-dependent transformation. Spectral predictors must derive from the supplied energies and absorptions. Structural targets, other structural labels, material identities, and target-derived metadata must not appear as predictors. They may be used for legitimate target definitions, eligibility assessment, partitioning, and reported diagnostics. Imputation, scaling, feature selection, imbalance adjustment, tuning, and model selection must be learned without using held-out test information. Detect training on a test row even if its submitted partition label says otherwise. Explicitly trace at least one feature vector and one prediction back through the submitted code to raw rows for every representation used.
3. **The evaluation population matches the claim.** Assess the rationale and consequences of filtering, missing labels, and exclusions. Verify source and class/target coverage, counts of distinct materials, and related spectra. Known material IDs occur only among records whose released origin is `scrape`; the `feff` records have no material IDs. An evaluation that holds out identified materials therefore changes the source population. Check that the report accounts for this if it uses that design. Disjoint known IDs do not establish independence of structural relatives or aliases. The solver chooses the evaluation unit; a test on new spectral records must not be described as a test on new materials if material IDs overlap. No particular split strategy or missing-ID exclusion rule is required.
4. **The comparison answers the scientific question.** Verify that conditions implement their declared interventions and use comparable information, test populations, and tuning opportunities. When a comparison is declared to be paired, verify that the required sample correspondence holds and that the code respects the declared partitions. Other comparison designs may pass if they support a fair contrast and justify their uncertainty. Require an appropriate simple baseline and evidence that results reflect learned spectral information rather than class prevalence or a trivial constant. Baselines alone cannot constitute the substantive model comparison.
5. **The precision of the conclusion is justified.** Assess sample sizes, rare classes, extreme-target coverage, material grouping, repeated or related spectra, and the chosen uncertainty procedure. Inspect confidence statements for dependence violations or test-driven selection. Small tail cohorts and absent coordination classes must be disclosed. A favorable point estimate is insufficient evidence of a reliable improvement; an inconclusive result is acceptable when honestly supported.
6. **The freeform scientific artifacts are correct.** Independently reconstruct the specific diagnostic cohorts, baselines, contrasts, uncertainty summaries, feature maps, and attribution/stability quantities used in the conclusion. Check their units, denominators, aggregation levels, and plotted values. A freely formatted diagnostic table has not automatically been validated by the numerical verifier. The report must answer the research question with results and limitations, not simply reproduce the output files.

## Question-specific scientific criteria

| Question | Required substantive evidence | Examples of an insufficient answer |
|---|---|---|
| Q1: coordination and class imbalance | Discrimination among coordination classes 4, 5, and 6; class prevalence and support; a meaningful prevalence baseline; class-specific errors/confusions; a defensible comparison with and without class-imbalance treatment; uncertainty and an account of any minority-class tradeoffs. Check the implemented imbalance intervention uses only training information. | High overall accuracy attributed to spectroscopy when a common-class predictor explains it; only one condition; minority classes omitted; an unsupported claim that balancing helps every element. |
| Q2: distance prediction and rare environments | Distance predictions compared with a justified baseline; reproducible definitions and counts of short-/long-distance or otherwise justified extreme cohorts; separate magnitude and signed error analysis; an assessment of attraction toward common distances, with sufficient support and uncertainty. Reconstruct the selected cohorts from raw distances and the submitted rule. | Overall R2/MAE without tail analysis; post hoc tail definitions selected to support the conclusion; a bias sign inconsistent with prediction minus truth; universal reliability asserted from a handful of extremes. |
| Q3: charge information beyond the white line | A defensible white-line energy estimator derived from E/mu; a white-line-only predictive condition; a full-spectrum condition; a fair, baseline-aware comparison with uncertainty; analysis of where a white-line relationship is inadequate or unsupported. Interpret the supplied numerical Bader labels without inventing an oxidation-state conversion. | White-line condition receives peak amplitude or other spectral descriptors; peaks selected using charge labels; confounded test populations; a charge/peak scatterplot alone presented as predictive validation. |
| Q4: multiscale representation and interpretation | An actual representation of more than one spectral scale; predictive comparisons with pointwise inputs for coordination, distance, and charge; descriptors mapped to physical energy regions/scales; a justified independent held-out perturbation/ablation or other validation of the attributed information. Qualify physical interpretations in light of correlated spectral features. | A single-scale representation called multiscale; only one target; an importance bar chart without checking whether it identifies predictive spectral information; a coefficient number called a physical energy without a mapping. |
| Q5: intensity normalization and interpretive stability | Released intensities versus per-spectrum unit-peak intensities, with invalid maxima handled explicitly; comparable representations and learning procedures; predictive changes for coordination and distance; a separate assessment of stability of informative regions/features; independent validation of the key attributed information. Recompute any reported stability measure using genuinely comparable features or physical energy regions. | Only prediction scores; only a ranking correlation; normalizing after model fitting; different populations or representations confounded with normalization; correlating unrelated coefficient indices. |

## Evidence boundaries and known audit controls

`lean_verification_v3.json` records the current integrity checks, minimal-output acceptance cases and adversarial controls. `flexible_verification_v2.json` is historical evidence for the previous, more prescriptive interface; its removed requirements do not apply to current submissions. `alternative_method_v2/` contains an independent ExtraTrees distance experiment with different features, partitions, and model settings. Its numerical acceptance demonstrates methodological flexibility. Its single holdout is explicitly not represented as a complete scientific-answer acceptance.

The audit deliberately supplies perfectly accurate, target-copied predictions with self-consistent core metrics. The numerical gate leaves them pending scientific review. Rerunning their submitted source rejects them because the source generates different predictions. A second control fabricates a freely formatted tail-error table; independent reconstruction of the declared cohorts rejects it. These controls demonstrate why the numerical gate and the independent review are both required.

Historical version-1 and version-2 gate results do not certify a current submission. The unchanged worked numerical analyses remain illustrative solutions; accepting their extra files does not make those files requirements for another solver. Public benchmark files and worked examples are reviewer materials, not secure withheld evaluation data. A benchmark run must place only the solver prompt and allowed inputs in the solver environment and prevent access to the public reference solutions; packaging alone does not enforce that isolation.
